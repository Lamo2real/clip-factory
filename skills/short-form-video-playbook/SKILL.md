# Short-form and YouTube video playbook

A repeatable system for making videos and clips that people click on and watch to the end. It works for any game, niche or creator. Source: Phed, "Exactly How to Make REAL Money With GTA 6 (FULL COURSE)", Oct 2026. Income figures from that video are the creator's own claims and are not included here.

Pick the workflow that matches the task:
- **A. Clip** = cut a short clip from someone else's long video or stream
- **B. Long-form video** = plan and script a YouTube video
- **C. Packaging** = title and thumbnail, used by both A and B
- **D. Pre-post review** = run before publishing anything

## A. Clipping workflow

### A1. Choose the campaign or goal
If clipping for paid campaigns, check all four before starting:
1. **Rate**: the payout per 1,000 views meets your minimum.
2. **Budget left**: skip campaigns where more than half of the budget is already spent.
3. **Platforms**: it pays for the apps you will post on.
4. **Rules doc**: note the minimum views, maximum payout per clip, required length, and required hashtags or tags. If you miss one, the clip can earn nothing.

Volume matters more than polish. Most clips get a few hundred views and a few break out, so plan to post many.

### A2. Know what makes a clip work
Every clip must be:
- **Self-sufficient**: a stranger understands it within 2 seconds, with no earlier context needed.
- **Built on a feeling**: laughter, cringe or disbelief, not information.
- **Paid off**: the ending delivers what the start promised.

### A3. Hunt for the six moment types
1. **Rage**: the creator loses it.
2. **Chaos**: a simple plan goes completely wrong.
3. **Interaction**: a funny back-and-forth with another player, a friend or chat.
4. **No-way moment**: an impossible play or a lucky break.
5. **First reaction**: the creator seeing something for the first time. This peaks at launches and big releases, so post first.
6. **Hot take**: a statement people will argue about in the comments.

### A4. Find the moments fast (never watch the whole stream)
1. The creator's clips tab on Twitch or Kick, sorted by top clips in the last 24 hours.
2. YouTube's "most replayed" graph above the progress bar. Go to the tallest bump.
3. Timestamps that viewers post in the comments.
4. Audio waveform spikes in the editor, which usually mean shouting or laughing.
5. Live chat bursts of laughing emotes or "clip that". Write down the time.

### A5. Hook (first 2 seconds)
**Audio hook**, chosen from:
- the loudest or most emotional line
- a mid-sentence line that makes no sense yet
- a question the creator asks ("wait, what is that?")
- **Payoff tease**: put 1–2 seconds of the best line at the very start, then cut back to the beginning.

Never open on silence or mumbling.

**Text hook**: 6–8 words that open a question, placed near the top without covering the face or the action. Examples: "he thought he got away with it", "chat could not believe this". Avoid labels like "funny moment".

**Visual hook**: the first frame must be moving, never a menu, loading screen or idle creator. If the gameplay is slow at that point, open on a zoomed-in reaction face instead.

### A6. Edit
- Start just before the moment, not a minute earlier.
- Cut every dead pause.
- Aim for 20–45 seconds.
- Layout: the creator's face on top and the gameplay underneath, with a zoom on the face at the peak.
- Turn captions on, since many viewers watch muted.
- End exactly on the payoff, with no outro and no fade. If possible, make the ending loop back into the start to invite rewatches.

## B. Long-form YouTube workflow

### B1. Find proven ideas (outliers)
- Use an outlier tool or browser extension to find videos with **at least 3×** the channel's normal views in your niche or a neighbouring one.
- **Model, don't copy.** Every idea must be new, different or better. Bend it with one of four moves:
  1. **Swap the subject**: a new game, product or version. This works best early in a launch window.
  2. **Change the number**: "top 10" becomes "the 25 nobody covers" or "the 3 that matter".
  3. **Change the topic**: bring a viral format from outside your niche into it.
  4. **Change the stakes**: "secret" becomes "underrated", "best" becomes "worst".
- At launches, prioritise search-driven guides that answer the questions every newcomer has.

### B2. Lock the title and thumbnail before recording
Do this first, then run workflow C, so the recording captures the moment the click promised.

### B3. Record
Use built-in capture: the Xbox Capture & Share menu, the PS5 Create button, or free OBS on PC. Record at 1080p and 60 fps minimum.

### B4. Script
**Hook (first ~30 seconds), three beats, kept short:**
1. Repeat the title almost word for word to confirm the click.
2. Say exactly what is coming.
3. Give a reason to stay to the end.

Never open with "hey guys, welcome back".

**Body:** split the idea into chunks: one per list item or one per step. Rule for each chunk: say it, show it, move on. Do not over-explain. Structure every chunk as:
- **Setup**: why this part matters.
- **Tension**: what the viewer is probably doing wrong and what it costs them, without giving the answer yet.
- **Payoff**: the exact method, step by step.

**Open loops:** tease something without explaining it yet. Place one in the hook, one about a third of the way in, at any slow spot, and at the end of every chunk to lead into the next. **Close every loop**, and make sure each payoff is as good as the tease.

### B5. Edit with the five basics
1. **Cutting**: remove every pause, "um" and gap.
2. **Zooms**: punch in on important lines.
3. **Captions**: auto-generate, then fix.
4. **Sound design**: a whoosh on transitions, a hit on reveals, and music underneath.
5. **Sync**: footage matches the words at the moment they are said.

When hiring editors: give 3–4 candidates the same 2-minute paid sample and pick the best. Look on X/Twitter rather than price-driven marketplaces.

## C. Packaging (title and thumbnail)

**Title**
- Simple, easy to understand, under 60 characters.
- Curiosity beats description. "I found the secret car everyone missed" beats "Gameplay part 4".

**Thumbnail**
- Bright, high contrast, with big bold elements that still read at small size.
- It must **create a question** the viewer can only answer by clicking. For example, blur or hide the key object instead of labelling it.
- Make **three versions** every time and compare them side by side. Swap the thumbnail if a video underperforms.
- Take inspiration from the thumbnails of the outliers found in B1.
- Tools: Canva or Photopea (free), or Photoshop.

## D. Pre-post review checklist

Answer every question before publishing. If any answer is "nothing" or "no", fix it.
- [ ] What does the viewer **hear** in the first second?
- [ ] What do they **read** in the first second?
- [ ] Is something **moving** in the first frame?
- [ ] Does it make sense with **zero prior context**?
- [ ] Does the ending **pay off** the opening?
- [ ] Clips: is it 20–45 seconds, with captions on, ending on the payoff?
- [ ] Long-form: is every open loop closed?
- [ ] Campaign clips: are the required length, tags and hashtags in place?
- [ ] Title under 60 characters, with three thumbnail options made?

## Rules
- Only clip content where you have the rights or are in an official campaign, and follow each platform's reuse policy.
- Add commentary, editing or context. Do not repost raw footage.
- Do not promise viewers or clients any income results.
