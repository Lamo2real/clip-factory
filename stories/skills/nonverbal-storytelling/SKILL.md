# Telling a story without words

- **One clear want.** In the first two seconds the viewer must see who the character is and what they want (a coffee,
  to get home, to stay on the road). Show it with a pose and a look, not text.
- **Faces carry the plot.** Change the expression at every turn of the story, using only the faces in that
  character's own list (for example calm → surprised → worried → relieved, when it has them). Hold each face long
  enough to read on a phone (at least half a second).
- **Big, simple acting.** One action at a time, exaggerated, facing the camera or in clear profile. A gesture reads
  better than a small movement.
- **Sound tells you how it feels.** A whoosh for speed, a skid for danger, a boing for silly, silence before a reveal.
  Character noises from the character's own list (a gasp, a phew, a giggle) replace lines of dialogue.
- **Bubbles are symbols.** ? for confusion, ! for surprise, a light bulb for an idea, sweat for nerves. Never words.
- **Captions are short and few.** Use on-screen text only for the lesson or a time jump, at most 6 words a line,
  never a description of what we already see.
- **Rule of three.** Try, fail, try differently, succeed. The third beat is the payoff.
- **End on a feeling.** A relieved face, a thumbs up, a funny button, then out.
